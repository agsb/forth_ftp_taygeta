
The Parable Of The Clay
by rj brown @ ecfb
 
   Once upon a time, long ago, there was a great and
powerful king that had just conqured a neighboring land.
In this land was a wonderful natural resource: a clay pit.
After first getting mired down in the stuff, the king
called his sourcerers and wise men.  They made the clay
useful by imposing structure on it.  They made bricks!
With the bricks, the kingdom went on a building binge.
They made a new palace for the Queen.  When she entered the
throne room, however, she determined that something was
missing.
 
   "How could anything be wrong, my dear," said the King.
   "Everything looks the same," she weeped.
 
   So the king called in his wise men again and they made
many beautiful arches with the bricks to form a ballistrade
down either side of the throne room.  But the queen was
still displeased.
 
   Meanwhile, a lowly potter in the poor section of the
kingdom had obtained some of the new material: the clay.
With it, he fashioned a beautiful Ming Vase, which he sent
to the Queen.  The queen weeped for joy.  "A totally new
structure has been discovered," she cried.  "It's
wonderful!"
 
   Moral:  Structure eases design by imposing restrictions.
           Don't impose them irrevokably, or you will loose
           something precious!
 
   Alegory:  The King is DOD, 
             the Queen is Ada,
             Charles Moore was the potter,
             Forth IS the clay!
