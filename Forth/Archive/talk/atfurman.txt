<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> for some kind of organization that would specialize in
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> professional issues and the commercialization of Forth.
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> FIG's constituency is a mixture of professionals and
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> hobbyists.  And many of those professionals didn't care
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> who admired Forth; they were in a position of having
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> discretion over programming language.  Whenever you have
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> a group of people together, you have to stick to things
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> that they are all interested in at the  same time, which
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> in FIG's case is Forth technology and sharing of ideas.
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> ...

<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> that would deal primarily with "professional" issues (so as
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> to complement FIG rather than compete)...
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> A few of us in the Silicon Valley chapter got together and
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> started to think about how to create such an organization..

<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> the thought of ACM's stature rubbing off on Forth was
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> great.  George Shaw really pursued the idea: he contacted
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> ACM and learned that they were surprisingly receptive.  He
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> jumped through the appropriate hoops and SIGForth was born.
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> ...
<[Alan F.] FIGGUEST> George has been the Char of SIGForth so far.  It has been a
