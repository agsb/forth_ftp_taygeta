<GARY-S>      The GEnie Forth RoundTable is pleased to welcome as
          tonight's guest, Jim Callahan, president of Harvard Softworks.

          been doing well programming big computers when he got seduced
          by ALTAIR, IMSI, and the IBM PC; only to be frustrated by a

          discovered Forth - an extensible incremental compiler that
          obsoleted even mainframe languages - or would give a decent
          implementation. OPPORTUNITY !
         
          He developed HS/FORTH to be fast, expandable, complete, and 
          bury the dinosaurs! The dinosaurs still roam beneath the gloss of
          turbothrusters and windows while Forth development continues at
          a pace Jim sees as slow. Ten years later He is still plugging away
          polishing, adding here and there, including OOP. Harvard Softworks
          Forth is the highly respected result. Please welcome Jim Callahan.

<[JIM] FIGGUEST> Thanks for the intro.
                 I'll begin by claiming that raw Forth has the
                 best potential (and about the worst features)
                 of any language I know. That mustard seed of
                 potential, however, lets you add ANYTHING that
                 you need for any given job, and add it just as
                 effectively as in languages dedicated to that
                 feature. Object Oriented Programming is one such
                 feature. The problem with OOL's is that they put
                 you in a straight jacket and complicate one part
                 of a program while they simplify another. Patches
                 for C and other languages, while they provide
                 useful features, are somewhat awkwardly integrated
                 and can in no way be optimized or extended for
                 specific situations. A Forth OOL can be customized
                 as needed, and exhibit negative overhead - the
                 program can become faster and more compact than
                 with conventional control structures, and much
                 more readable. We regard OOL as the first real step
                 toward a natural language computer language --
                 we've made it all the way to:  subject verb
                 Next? 
<[Len] NMORGENSTERN> Could you give us an example of the syntax that
                     your OOP system uses?
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> Sure, let's assume you want to control a robot,
                 much more general than windows. ..
                     10 paces ALVIN FORWARD
                 or a complex robot with multiple arms (inherited of
                 course)    10 degrees ALVIN 's RIGHT-ARM RAISE
                 or control a whole expedition:
                          FOR-ALL MAR 's robot ADVANCE
                 etc, piled higher and deeper.  
<[Len] NMORGENSTERN> Thanx
<GARY-S> Jim - this implies HS Forth/OOP can multitask
         or multiuser or both ... can it and can it control say a
         gang of steppers requiring precision movements all at once ?
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> Yes HS/FORTH can multitask, and multi-user,
                 although the examples cited don't necessarily
                 imply that. Simultaneous precision movement
                 also depends on hardware availability. That
                 which is there is necessarily somewhat sequential. 
<[Dennis] D.RUFFER> Jim, how deeply have you embedded your OO features into 
                    Forth itself?
                    Do you do any of your basics with them?
                    and have they helped?
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> We would like eventually to do a rewrite because there
                 are many internal areas that would benefit greatly.
                 Currently the FOOPS package is a 3 page file that you
                 load when needed. 
<[Dennis] D.RUFFER> What areas do you think would benefit then?
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> File I/O certainly, the windowing and graphics package,
                 and multitasking. The standard Forth user variable basis
                 for multitasking is most inadequate. Could also get into
                 math and formulas - but by then it isn't looking much
                 like Forth anymore. The only feature we'd never consider
                 scrapping is interactive incremental compilation itself.
<GARY-S> Since Forth is synchronous in nature what method do you employ for 
         multitasking - round-robin would be less painful but harder to 
         control I would think.
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> HS/FORTH supports both round robin and interrupt driven
                 multitasking. There are, of course, some reasonable limits
                 on what may be done in the interrupt driven tasks. For
                 instance, if the PAD is to be used, the interrupt driven
                 task must have its own. 
<[Dennis] D.RUFFER> Jim, could you tell us more about the features of your OOP?
                    i.e. does it have late binding, etc.
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> We believe that language features should be enabling
                 rather than confining.  Let the programmer or manager
                 create the restrictions as appropriate given experience
                 and the situation. Along those lines, FOOPS allows you
                 to encapsulate your objects. It makes sure that only
                 valid messages are accepted, and only valid methods
                 executed. Private data is just that, private - and
                 inheriting objects can only directly access that data
                 if an appropriate method has been created. There are
                 however, many ways to send messages to objects. First
                 are the simple syntactical outlines presented earlier.
                 It is also possible to convert messages to id numbers
                 or even method addresses and directly apply them to
                 objects. Very useful for some table driven problems and
                 for extensions like the FOR-ALL modifier.  I could go
                 on till dawn, but I'd better give someone else a turn. 
<GARY-S> FOOPS is obviously a superset of HS/Forth - or an application driven 
         from HS/Forth. What does that do to HS/Forth's vaunted reputation as 
         a speedy kernel ?
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> Since the kernel isn't modified, it's just as fast as ever, 
                 and the FOOPS extension is very efficiently organized so 
                 that its execution has very little overhead. Usually less than
                 doing the same routine with conventional logic. If you try to
                 use it for something that is not naturally represented as 
                 objects, perhaps a set of differntial equations, there might
                 be some small overhead, but not much.If I looked at it closer,
                 even diffy q's might benefit. Who knows?

<[ROBERT] R.GESSWEIN1> Do you have or need garbage collection, constructors/
                       destructors and all that typical "OO" stuff?
                       Are objects created on the fly and left on the stack 
                       as parameters?
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> Garbage collection is usually (NOT ALWAYS) an excuse
                 for sloppy programming. It comes from the enslavement
                 of OOL to those ephemeral windows ( 2 dimensional
                 hierarchically limited robots with limited ancestries).
                 However, it is possible to reuses objects, and ones
                 that have been discarded could easily be put in a discard
                 linked list. For those few situations that genuinely? benefit
                 from garbage collection, we will include an add on utility
                 that efficiently performs that function shortly.  
<GARY-S> FOOPS, as you describe it, follows the PARC Smalltalk model rather 
         closely - how close ? (approximations are fine <grin>)
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> I've only briefly looked  at Smalltalk. I never could stand
                 the straight jacket - and Smalltalk goes for a "cute" syntax
                 that I couldn't much tolerate. I'm sure the underlying ideas
                 are similar but I prefer a straightforward and extensible
                 syntax.  
<GARY-S> You mentioned subject verb order earlier - could you amplify on that
         a bit ?
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> From some of the talk I've heard various places I think
                 that some people are confusing "object" as a thing with
                 "object" of a sentence. I think our understanding and
                 future extendability would be better served avoiding that
                 and considering the "object" to be the "subject" of the
                 sentences we write. Considering it the object is basically
                 egocenter - the progammer/program becomes the subject, and
                 that is very limiting once we need to go to the next step
                 and write not merely: (not FOOPS)   RAISE ARM
                 but:   (FOOPS some day)   ALVIN 's RIGHT-ARM RAISE CUP AND 
                                           DRINK
<[Len] NMORGENSTERN> I think that this may match your concept
                     Object: Anything that may be apprehended intellectually
                     (Random House Dictionary)
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> Seems to me verbs can be apprehended intellectually. Things
                 must have a cerain degree of specificity, as well as
                 generality. There is more danger, and more immediate utility,
                 in being to specific. The enslavement of OOL to windows is
                 a good case in point. Windows can survive without multiple
                 inheritance support, and with half baked multiple inheritance
                 support -- and that has been one of the major flaws of
                 early OOLs. 
<GARY-S> Closing comments please, Jim
<[JIM] FIGGUEST> I think we have only scratched the surface (global
                 comment) in the evolution of OOL's, much as we
                 have only scratched the surface in the evolution of
                 Forth. In both cases most people are so busy trying
                 to use the part that's already available to accomplish
                 something that is needed immediately that there is no
                 concerted effort to finish writing the tools. Most
                 people fundamentally interested in the tools lack the
                 organization to really forge for a long distance into
                 new ground - and few individuals can really accomplish
                 what an organized and well directed group can. In
                 any case, I predict that the OOL path will have us
                 programming  in something closely resembling natural
                 language and clearly based on Forth within a few years.
                 enuf said, and thanks for the invitation.
